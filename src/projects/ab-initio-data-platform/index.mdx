## Outcome

1. Shipped a new product in **six months**, on time, as a customer release for a European bank.
2. Unified a **3-product** wrangling workflow into **1** platform: find → transform → schedule.
3. Led the design-token effort for our first design system: **600 variables → 150 scalable tokens**.

## The problem: fragmented, overwhelming, inconsistent

After working through the products myself and talking with PM, eng leads, and adjacent teams, I framed the problems as:

1. **Fragmented.** Context was lost every time users switched products.
2. **Overwhelming.** Each product exposed far more than most people needed for the job.
3. **Inconsistent.** With no design system, each product had its own interaction model.

This problem stung as it had already lost Ab Initio deals purely based on UX. The company needed to prove it could ship a UX-led product under real customer constraints without waiting for a multi-year rebuild.

## My role: prove UX can win in six months, for one customer

My PM owned the multi-year vision: fold eight products into one platform with shared workflows like data acquisition, masking, wrangling, and issue management. Engineering owned the architecture problems.

My role was to turn that vision into concrete flows we could ship, as the sole designer with 1 PM, 2 eng leads, and about 15 developers. We aligned on three principles:

1. **Unified.** Support the end-to-end workflow in one platform instead of three products.
2. **Curated.** Tailor the experience to user goals instead of exposing every tool.
3. **Familiar.** Make the workflow feel consistent as we folded products together.

## Making painful cuts to scope a 6 month MVP

With six months and a huge product suite behind us, scope was the real design problem. I led weekly reviews with PM and eng leads to force the hard cuts:

- What is the core workflow this customer needs?
- Which pieces of Metadata>Portal, Express>It, and Control>Center have to transfer for that workflow?
- How bespoke should a one-customer MVP be versus a prototype of the larger vision?

We scoped to data wrangling for two primary users: analysts who transform and publish data, and consumers who browse finished outputs.

## Navigating ambiguity without data

The organization was just starting to invest in UX, so we had no user research practice and almost no direct access to end users. I learned the products from documentation, met with teams who had already solved adjacent problems, and kept tight 1:1s with my PM and eng leads. Occasional TAM feedback at milestones was the closest thing we had to customer signal.

That still left a lot of unknowns. So I wrote down the research questions I would have asked, the hypotheses behind my assumptions, and kept moving. Making the evidence gap visible stopped it from becoming a recurring fight in every review:

1. Walk me through the last dataset you prepared end to end. Where did you stop and wait on someone else?
2. When you switch from Metadata>Portal to Express>It, what do you carry over by hand?
3. What makes you trust a dataset that someone else built?


## Leading the design token initiative

Ab Initio had no formal design system, so every product had its own styles and patterns. As part of standing up Honeycomb, our first design system, I led the design tokens effort from planning through alignment, implementation, and distribution, which took about **four months** to ship.

We started from a shared color file of nearly **600 variables** with low adoption and no path to scale, and through my leadership we created a system of **150 scalable tokens** tied to accessibility, theming, and scalability.

## Impact: three products → one

I designed the end-to-end wrangling experience that shipped on time for the bank release. In one product, users could seamlessly complete a workflow that used to be a 3-product, fractured experience.

We did not have adoption dashboards or CSAT for this release. What we did have:

1. **Customer adoption.** At the European bank, the MVP replaced the old three-product flow.
2. **On-time release.** Six months, shipped for a real customer - not an internal demo.
3. **Foundation.** The tokens I led shipped in Honeycomb and carried into that broader work.
4. **Org shift.** The MVP evolved into the Ab Initio Data Platform - the company-wide mission every team aligned on.

## Looking back

I treated the customer as a consumer instead of a partner. Next time I would bring them into scoping and requirements from day one, not only major milestones.
