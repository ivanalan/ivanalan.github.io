## The problem: “When are you free?”

Every 4 days, my partner has a 24 hour overnight shift at the hospital. The catch is the pattern: a 4 day cycle does not sit cleanly on a 7 day calendar.

This schedule is not as easy as saying “I always work Mondays and Thursdays,” and is hard to share with friends and family. So everyone asks the same question: “when are you free?”

<ShiftCalendar />

## Design bet: sharing

Most apps only focus on creating a recurring schedule for yourself. The real job was making “when are you free?” answerable without another back and forth.

So, I built the product around sharing. If someone already has DeGuardia, it opens with the rotation ready to save. If they don’t, the same page still lets them export to Google Calendar, download an .ics, or download an image.

<ShareEmbed />

## Designing beyond just a recurring schedule.

The needs I found went further, and where my app outshines other trackers/schedulers:

1. Counting down the shifts and days left in a brutal rotation
2. Seeing when the one free weekend a month actually falls, so you can plan rest
3. Overlaying a friend’s schedule against your own

## My process: iterating over planning

When I started, I planned a lot upfront: information architecture, requirements, edge cases, libraries. While the IA held, almost everything else changed while building.

With AI, reversing tech choices is cheap. So, I learned to plan foundational pieces but start developing sooner.

## What I’d do differently

I shipped rotation creation, then visualization in a calendar, then sharing. But this was the wrong priority, as I should have shipped “sharing” immediately. That mistake clarified how a PRD should take priority over engineering instincts to build out a robust system first, then add in sharing/collaboration.
