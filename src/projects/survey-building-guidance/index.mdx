## Impact

1. Grew guidance monthly active usage from **10% → 35%**, with total events up **600%**.
2. Co-designed **one** shared Error / Warning / Tip pattern that shipped in **two** products.
3. Replaced an opaque health score with a severity framework built for future AI agents.

## The problem: low usage and hard to scale

Expert Review had low usage (~11% MAU) and wasn’t scalable. Increasing adoption would move three business levers:

1. **Time-to-value**: faster path to realizing value
2. **Platform maturity**: nudge toward advanced features
3. **Fewer support tickets**: catch errors before publish

I owned problem framing, the business opportunity, and success metrics, and worked with the eng lead to design a scalable error system.

## Three blockers: untrustworthy, unclear, out of context

Amplitude and existing research pointed to three blockers:

1. **Untrustworthy score:** Amplitude showed that Expert Review scores were inaccurate, and builders found it untrustworthy and confusing.”
2. **Unclear priority:** users found it “hard to parse” so much information

## A severity framework instead of a health score

I replaced the health score with a severity model reused from our banner pattern: Error, Warning, and Tip.

The hard call was which issues should be errors, blocking publish. We reserved Errors for a small, listed set of high-risk problems that make data collection impossible; everything else stayed a Warning or Tip so authors could still save and iterate on later.

## Designing at scale by collaborating with research

At Qualtrics scale, shipping a pattern is expensive to undo. I partnered with a researcher to run a quick study to answer some still-open questions and run usability tests.

<CaseImage
  client:load
  src="/survey-building-guidance/assets/research-interaction-concepts.png"
  alt="Three interaction concepts for survey guidance: modal, left pane, and right-hand side panel"
  caption="Three concepts tested. The right-hand panel won."
  bgClassName="bg-secondary"
/>

Two decisions came out of that research:

1. **Only new metrics would be eligible as Errors.** Because users were “publishing” as a way to “save” their survey, there was a huge risk in suddenly blocking users from publish.
2. **Move forward with a right-hand side panel.** We tested a banner, a pop-up, and a right panel. The right panel won as it was easy to keep in context while editing.

## Advocating to create a copy system that scales

Metric copy was inconsistent and one-off. So, I created a scalable content system instead: what was flagged, why it matters, and what to do.

## Looking back

Looking back, I would have pulled engineering into the framework earlier as co-designers, not late reviewers of a proposal. The UI work was comparatively straightforward, while the real bottleneck was the framework.

I would have also pushed the problem framing towards building an AI-agent harness. The side panel was right for builders now, but we need guardrails for future agentic survey building.
